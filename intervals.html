How to practise so it works on real music
1. Degrees matter more than intervals. An interval alone is two notes with no key. In real music, every note has a job in its key. The same minor 3rd sounds different as 1→♭3 and as 6→1. That is why Degrees is the main exercise here: a cadence sets the key, then you name the degree of one note.

2. Sing. Sing what you hear out loud, even if you only hum. Singing a note fixes it in your ear much faster. You need no microphone: sing after you answer, along with the resolution.

3. Ten minutes a day beats an hour a week. Hearing is memory, and memory loves short, frequent practice.

4. Move on when an item is steady. Check the Ear map. Aim for 90–95% on every item, not just on average.

5. Work out music by ear. The trainer is a warm-up, not real practice. Transcribe the bass line of a favourite track: nothing trains your ear better.
